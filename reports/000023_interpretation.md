# Experiment 23: two dated full-post replays recovered; independent review remains pending

The bounded pilot recovers **two dated archival post bodies and both publisher PDFs**, providing substantially more FL-side material than the earlier partial indexed excerpts. Both native-site requests still fail with TLS protocol errors. The archived responses are historical evidence: they do not establish current-site contents, independent word meanings or untouched test status.

All eight permitted primary requests execute: two native FL requests, two publisher PDF requests, two exact-URL archive metadata requests and two metadata-selected raw replays. No infrastructure diagnostic or outcome-dependent retry occurs. Every failed native attempt, response body, header, error and archive selection is preserved. The whole-post nominees and publisher windows were fixed before fetching; no copied name, word-count equality or matching score selected them.

The word counts cover the entire extracted post body, including mixed-language prefaces, headings, superscripts and references. They are not counts of verified Kamri words or accepted aligned tokens. The archive replies have a single post body, the frozen post title, a bound original permalink, and replay/Memento dates agreeing with the selected metadata record. Both metadata queries requested only the latest exact-URL HTTP-200 HTML record before the registration cutoff; no alternative timestamp or fallback host was selected after seeing content.

Two independent source-material issues remain open and explicit:

- The new Western publisher PDF has a different SHA-256 from the earlier saved PDF. Both parse as 357 pages, and the recorded producer is the same, but their creation-date metadata differs. This does not establish that metadata is the only change or that the edition changed. The registered exact-byte requirement fails, so earlier normalized source prose is withheld. The dossier retains the new PDF and all five fixed page images for an extraction/content-equivalence review.
- A descriptive post-capture fingerprint check finds that Western's received content-decoded archive body agrees with the index's SHA-1/base32 digest, while Risk's does not. This is an unresolved payload/provenance/representation discrepancy; no visible-prose alteration or exact WARC-payload equivalence is asserted. Original bytes are preserved, and the issue is prominently included in the Risk dossier. The check did not change the preregistered material gate or select a favorable retry. A passing material gate is procedural readiness for review, not semantic acceptance.

The registered result is therefore zero current raw-FL posts, two identity-confirmed dated archival post materials, two available publisher windows, one source-prose selection bound to prior bytes, and one of two packets passing the full material-readiness gate. Both dossiers are useful: Western needs source-prose/provenance review, and Risk has paired materials with an archival-fingerprint issue to resolve. Neither dossier accepts labels. Imported indexed excerpts and author transcriptions were not substituted for the raw captures.

All eight fixed publisher pages were rendered and visually inspected. Prose, headings, page labels and footnotes are legible; page-edge continuation and the limited source window remain visible. This is same-assistant visual QA, not independent semantic review. A fixed publisher window does not establish correspondence or source coverage for an entire FL post. Both unchanged downloaded PDFs are supplied in the dossiers; no PDF content was authored or edited.
