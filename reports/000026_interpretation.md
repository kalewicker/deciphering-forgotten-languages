# Experiment 26: useful restricted retrieval; copied-anchor specificity gate fails

All three primary clusters uniquely retrieve the nominated publisher export within the fixed eleven-document development corpus. All three abstain when that export is removed. However, each cluster also nominates an unrelated, equal-length synthetic passage containing the anchors in the same order when the actual export is absent. **The combined source-specificity gate fails.** The experiment supports restricted-corpus retrieval by these clues; it does not admit an immediate source identity, a translation or a word meaning.

## What the controls establish

Each primary cluster's ordered frame has exactly the nominated window's whitespace-word length, with only its registered anchors placed among `xqframe` filler. All three achieve the same complete score as the nominated source. With the source present, the rule abstains on the tie; with it absent, the rule nominates the frame. This demonstrates that the representation cannot distinguish genuine surrounding source text from copied anchors.

These deliberately constructed lures are an identifiability test. Their 3/3 success is **not an estimate of the prevalence of false attribution in real sources**. The failure does not disprove a relationship between the actual FL and publisher material. It blocks upgrading this anchor-only procedure into source-identity evidence under the registered criterion.

Reversing the three Western labels lowers each score from (3,3) to (3,1); reversing the Risk pair lowers (2,2) to (2,1). Masking all registered anchor occurrences makes all three scores (0,0). In the single fixed Risk counterfactual, whole-token replacement of `possibile` with `possible` and `possibilites` with `possibilities` also makes the spelling-query score (0,0). The original publisher and FL bytes are unchanged. These diagnostics confirm what the rule responds to; they supply no meanings for adjacent unknown tokens.

The natural panel is also easy: all three descriptive single-anchor queries uniquely retrieve their nominated export, including the technical phrase and lone shared vocabulary word. No other export contains even one complete anchor for any registered query. Therefore the natural 3/3 result cannot establish rarity, web-wide specificity or robustness against closely related source passages. Same-work alternatives outside the existing exports and independently chosen topical near-neighbors are absent. Query selection occurred after inspecting these pairs. No significance level, population error rate or held-out accuracy is reported.

Experiment 26 stops at this failure, as registered. The next separately scoped diagnostic should test **observable text evidence outside the reported anchors**, with same-work or topical alternatives that challenge the nominated source. Scope any claim to what those observations establish. A revised anchor weight or extra unrelated documents would not address the copied-frame limitation. Source/passage context and extraction boundaries must be explicit; a semantic claim would still require independently assessable evidence beyond copied forms. Do not infer the English source's sentence meanings for the unknown FL text.
